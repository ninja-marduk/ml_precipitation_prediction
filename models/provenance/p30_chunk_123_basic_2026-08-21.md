## The six-cell ranking reverses between two seeds

Mean R2 over the twelve horizons:

| variant | bundle | seed 42 | seed 123 | spread |
|---------|--------|--------:|---------:|-------:|
| GAT | BASIC | 0.3882 | **0.4952** | **0.107** |
| GAT | PAFC | 0.5374 | 0.4846 | 0.053 |
| GCN | BASIC | 0.4387 | 0.3652 | 0.074 |
| GCN | PAFC | 0.4869 | 0.4251 | 0.062 |
| SAGE | BASIC | 0.4480 | 0.4483 | **0.0003** |
| SAGE | PAFC | 0.4715 | 0.4366 | 0.035 |

seed 42: GAT/PAFC > GCN/PAFC > SAGE/PAFC > SAGE/BASIC > GCN/BASIC > GAT/BASIC
seed 123: GAT/BASIC > GAT/PAFC > SAGE/BASIC > SAGE/PAFC > GCN/PAFC > GCN/BASIC

**GAT with BASIC features is last of six at one seed and first of six at the other.**
Its spread, 0.107, is the widest in the table and is the same cell the archived
factorial already flagged as the most seed-sensitive: the archived three-seed
standard deviation for it is 0.1068, and the corrected two-seed spread is 0.1070.
Two pipelines, the same instability, which makes it a property of the configuration
rather than of either run.

**The bundle effect is not stable either.** At seed 42 all three PAFC cells sat
above all three BASIC cells, which looked like a clean main effect. At seed 123 they
interleave, with GAT/BASIC first and GCN/BASIC last. One seed would have supported
either story.

SAGE with BASIC is the opposite case: 0.4480 and 0.4483, a spread of 0.0003 across
two independent initialisations.

## What this is evidence for

This is the manuscript's own thesis, measured on the corrected pipeline rather than
inherited from the archived one. A ranking of six configurations, produced by a
correct temporal split on a leakage-free graph with released code, reverses
end to end when the random seed changes. Nothing in a single-run table would show
it, and the configuration that moves most is the one the companion study reported
as its headline.

The third seed is what turns this into a factorial with paired uncertainties rather
than two orderings that disagree. Until then the honest statement is that the
orderings disagree, not that either is right.
